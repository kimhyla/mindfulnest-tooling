## The Script

### WELCOME
Ahh... you've come to send your Kindness magic. Good.

### CONNECTION
You saw the kind words travel as glowing bubbles — how both you and the Sweetrose got brighter. Now it's your turn to feel that warmth inside.

### SETUP
Let everything go still for a moment. Feel yourself right here.

Now, if you want to, put your hand right on your heart. You can feel it beating there. Think of who you want to send your warmth to — the Sweetrose, or someone from your life you care about.

Here's what I want you to do.

### INSTRUCTION
Breathe in slowly... and think of something good for them. Something that would make them really happy.

Now breathe out... and send it to them. Feel the warmth that goes with it.

Let's do it again. Same good thing. Breathe in... hold it in your heart...

...and breathe out... send it again.

One more time. In... feel it...

...and out... send it with all your warmth.

### DEEPENING
Keep sending that same good thing. In... hold it...

...and out... send it.

Here's the secret. That good thing you keep sending... check inside for a second. Are you feeling a little warmer and nicer too?

Keep going. In... feel it...

...and out... send it... and feel that warmth inside you at the same time.

That's the double-up. Your kind thoughts go out to the other person... and they also make you feel nicer and warmer too. That's the Kindness magic working.

### LANDING
That warm feeling right there... inside you... that's your Kindness magic.

### EXIT
Stay right there. Just keep sending.

## Voice Direction Notes

**Welcome:** The "Ahh..." is a warm, pleased exhale — the narrator has been waiting for this moment. "Good." is delivered with quiet satisfaction and genuine warmth. Different energy from M1 "Good." — this one is softer, more tender.

**Connection:** References Phase A vocabulary directly ("glowing bubbles," "got brighter") to anchor the watch→try shift. Frames the transition from external (bubbles traveling) to internal (feeling warmth inside). Not rushed — gives space for the child to recognize the shift.

**Setup:** "Let everything go still for a moment. Feel yourself right here." — grounding beat (van der Kolk, Gilbert). Settles the child's nervous system before asking them to generate warmth. Without this, some children attempt warmth-sending from a still-activated state, producing head-only kindness. "If you want to, put your hand right on your heart" — offers the hand-on-heart anchor as OPTIONAL. Not every child will feel comfortable with direct hand-on-heart pressure; the phrasing "if you want to" honors that. "Think of who you want to send your warmth to — the Sweetrose, or someone from your life you care about." — opens the target choice. Preserves narrative continuity for children who want it while enabling real-life transfer for children who are ready. The choice is offered without pressure; either target is equally valid. "Here's what I want you to do" shifts to action.

**Instruction:** First breath pairs with forming a kind wish — "think of something good for them." The child's ACTION is wishing, not warming. Warmth is the CONSEQUENCE: "Feel the warmth that goes with it" — spoken with genuine surprise, as if the warmth is a bonus the child discovers. Extended exhale gets verbal space for the sending. Each cycle simplifies: first cycle is fully guided, second drops to "think of another good thing," third shortens to "something good." The child takes ownership of the rhythm. "To them" refers back to whoever the child chose in SETUP — works for the Sweetrose or a real person/pet. Second cycle repeats the pattern. Third cycle simplifies slightly — "In... and feel any warmth growing" acknowledges that warmth might build across repetitions.

**Deepening:** "Keep sending just like that" — child owns the rhythm now. Cycles continue with "another good thing" — the wishing is now automatic, the child barely needs prompting. TURNING POINT: "All those good things you're sending... notice what's happening inside you." The pivot is from outward (what you're sending) to inward (what you're feeling). "Check inside for a second. Are you feeling a little warmer and nicer too?" — genuine curiosity, not a leading question. The child is invited to look inward and report what they find. The answer might be yes, might be no — both are fine. We don't assume the child's internal state. "That's the double-up" — names the phenomenon using Phase A vocabulary. "Your kind words are making them feel good... and making you feel warm too" — the complete mechanism stated simply: wishes go OUT, warmth comes BACK. Both directions named explicitly. Final line: "That's the Kindness magic" — quiet certainty, ownership.

**Landing:** "That warm feeling right there... inside you..." — soft, almost a whisper, very specific location (inside). "That's your Kindness magic." — quiet certainty, ownership language. Different from M1's "That calm" — here we name the felt sensation precisely.

**Exit:** "Stay right there. Just keep sending." — trails off. Does not conclude. Opens space for the Rescue sustain.

## Clinical Grounding

### Technique
Externally-directed loving-kindness (metta) activating prosocial emotion and parasympathetic response. Kind-word generation and heart-warmth visualization (felt sense, not visual imagery) activate the parasympathetic nervous system via vagal tone and release of oxytocin (Fredrickson, Gilbert, Neff). The "double-up" discovery — simultaneous warmth outward AND inward — reflects the neuroscience of prosocial emotion: the giver receives the same benefit as the receiver (Singer & Klimecki, Neff & Germer).

### Design Decisions

1. **Hand-on-heart is OPTIONAL** — "If you want to" — some children find direct chest pressure uncomfortable or triggering (trauma-informed practice)
2. **No visualization language** — "feel the warmth" not "imagine golden light" or "see the light traveling" (Neff & Germer, somatic-first approach)
3. **Felt sense over visualization** — warmth is physiologically real (oxytocin, vagal response); child generates the sensation themselves, not through mental imagery
4. **Conditional language throughout** — "notice if," "notice what's happening" not "you will feel," "you will get warmer" (Salzberg, Kornfield — permission, not promise)
5. **The "double-up" discovery is the DEEPENING moment** — sudden realization that sending warmth makes YOU warmer too (Gilbert, Singer & Klimecki — prosocial emotion benefits giver AND receiver equally)
6. **Open question-based verification** — "Check inside for a second. Are you feeling a little warmer and nicer too?" invites the child to look inward without assuming what they'll find. Not a leading question — the answer can be yes or no. We never tell the child what they're feeling. (Hanson, child agency; Salzberg, Kornfield on permission)
7. **Grounding beat before warmth practice** — "Let everything go still... Feel yourself right here." Van der Kolk and Gilbert both recommend settling the nervous system before asking it to generate warmth. Without grounding, children may produce head-only kindness from a still-activated state.
8. **Target choice offered in SETUP** — child chooses Sweetrose (narrative continuity) OR someone from their real life (real-world transfer). Both are equally valid. This resolves the portability tension: the technique works outside the app because the child has already practiced it with a real target. (Germer: concrete target; Salzberg: permission)
9. **Externally-directed (not self-compassion)** — loving-kindness toward the Sweetrose creature or chosen real-life target, not the self. Self-compassion comes later (Arc 9). This is altruistic warmth (Salzberg, Neff & Germer early-stage scaffolding)
8. **Silence for warmth-generation** — gaps after "notice any warmth that's already there" and after "send that warmth" allow the child to generate warmth without narrative guidance (Thich Nhat Hanh, spacious instruction)
9. **Extended exhale emphasis via pacing** — more verbal space on out-breath (Porges vagal mechanism, applied to emotional warmth)

## Notes for Kim Review

1. **Voice warmth:** This script should feel noticeably warmer than M1. The "Ahh..." in Welcome is softer, more tender. The deepening moment ("You're getting warmer too, aren't you?") should land as genuine surprise/delight, not clinical explanation.

2. **Hand-on-heart phrasing:** The "If you want to" language is intentional — some children have trauma responses to direct chest pressure or feel self-conscious about heart-awareness at this age. The protocol offers the anchor without mandating it.

3. **Double-up discovery:** The script positions the child's self-warming as a DISCOVERY moment, not a promised outcome. "You're getting warmer too, aren't you?" is a question that invites the child to verify their own experience. This honors the child's agency and avoids the failure mode of the narrator "promising" feelings that don't materialize.

4. **Comparison to M1:** M1 is physical/mechanical (belly rises/falls, feels calming). M4 is emotional/warm (warmth generation, care for another, simultaneous self-benefit). Different operating logic, different body system. The narrator's energy should shift accordingly.

5. **Phase A vocabulary fidelity:** All Phase A terms are preserved exactly: "kind words," "glowing bubbles," "both you and the Sweetrose got brighter," "warmth," "Kindness magic," "double-up," "send."

6. **Preceding/following context:** The Phase A bridge ("Ready to try it for real?") and transition ("Now close your eyes...") are already in the jsx. The Rescue sustain ("Hold onto that warmth... keep sending it...") will follow. This script bridges those moments seamlessly.

Please review and provide feedback. Happy to adjust voice direction, script phrasing, or clinical framing based on your input.
