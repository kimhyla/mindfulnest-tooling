## The Script

### WELCOME
Ahh... you've come to learn the magic of Focus. Good.

### CONNECTION
You saw how thoughts are like clouds — and you have the magical power to choose which ones get your attention. Let's sharpen that power.

### PREVIEW
In a moment I will ask you to watch for a thought.
And soon after that, a thought will appear in your mind.
When your thought appears, you will recognize it.
You will say, "ah-ha! I found a thought!"
You will notice what the thought is about.
And then you will let it go right away.
Don't pay it any more attention after that.

### SETUP
Let's begin. Sit very still — and imagine you're lying down on a soft grass — looking up at a big, wide, open sky.

### INSTRUCTION
Now ask yourself — I wonder what my next thought will be?

*[SPACER A — 8 seconds. Child watches for thoughts. Ambient bed + silence.]*

### CHECK-IN
Have you noticed one?

*[SPACER B — 5 seconds. Child continues observing.]*

### GUIDED PRACTICE
When you notice one, say, "ah-ha! I had a thought!"
Notice what the thought is about. Then stop thinking about it.
Put your attention back on the job of watching thoughts.

*[SPACER C — 8 seconds. Child practices independently.]*

### LANDING
Watching your thoughts makes you stronger and stronger.

### EXIT
Stay right there. Just keep watching...

## What Changed from v1 Draft (and Why It's Better)

### 1. The Preview Rule (NEW SECTION TYPE)
v1 dropped the child into observation ("just notice it... like a cloud in your sky") and hoped for the best. v2 **walks through every step of the exercise before starting:** "I will ask you to watch → a thought will appear → you will recognize it → you will say ah-ha → you notice what it's about → you let it go." The child gets a complete mental map before the territory.

**Why it works:** This is the Phase A "I'll show you, then you try for real" principle applied *within* Phase B. For cognitive/mental-skill modules where the practice is invisible (no hand on belly, no counting), the child needs to rehearse the action sequence verbally before doing it eyes-closed.

### 2. The "Ah-Ha!" Action Marker
v1 used passive language: "just notice it." v2 gives the child a **specific playful thing to DO** at the defusion moment: say "ah-ha! I found a thought!" This IS the defusion — it creates the gap between thinker and thought — but it feels like a game of spotting, not therapy.

**Why it works:** Concrete action > abstract instruction. "Say ah-ha" passes the Body/Explain Test instantly. A 7-year-old can tell a friend: "When I see a thought, I say ah-ha!"

### 3. Job Framing
"Put your attention back on the **job** of watching thoughts." Roles are concrete. Observation is abstract. Kids understand jobs.

### 4. "Sharpen" > "Discover"
"Let's sharpen that power" is active, empowering, builds on Phase A's magic vocabulary. "Let's discover something" was passive and vague.

### 5. "Stronger and Stronger" Landing
v1: "That stillness... that watching... that's your Focus magic." (passive naming)
v2: "Watching your thoughts makes you stronger and stronger." (active power-building)
The child ends feeling like they're building a skill, not just sitting in a state.

### 6. Instruction-Heavy, Not Atmosphere-Heavy
v1 spent significant word budget on atmosphere (grass, sky, spaciousness, discovery beat). v2 compresses atmosphere to one sentence and spends that budget on crystal-clear instruction. The Preview section IS the investment — telling the child exactly what to do.

## New Authoring Rules Extracted

These should be added to MODULE_AUTHORING_GUIDE for future modules:

### Rule: Preview Before Practice (Mental-Skill Modules)
For modules where the practice is cognitive/invisible (no physical anchor like hand-on-belly), add a PREVIEW section between CONNECTION and SETUP that walks through every step of the exercise in sequence. The child should be able to predict what happens next before "Let's begin."

**Applies to:** M3 (Thought Clouds), M7 (Brave Steps), M8 (Worry Box), M13 (Sleepy Stargazing) — any module where the child can't observe their own body doing the technique.

**Does NOT apply to:** M1 (Belly Breathing), M2 (4-7-8), M4 (Mindful Listening), M9-M12 (body-based modules) — physical anchors make the practice self-evident.

### Rule: Action Marker at Therapeutic Moment
Give the child a specific phrase or micro-action to perform at the key therapeutic moment. The marker should be:
- Playful (not clinical)
- Speakable (child can say it internally)
- Self-reinforcing (doing it IS the skill)

Examples: "Ah-ha!" for defusion, "Found it!" for sense anchor, "Squeeze!" for PMR.

### Rule: Job Framing for Observation Tasks
When the child's task is to observe/watch/notice, frame it as "the job of [watching/listening/noticing]." Jobs are concrete and give the child a role. Passive observation is abstract.

### Rule: Sharpen > Discover for Returning Skills
When a Phase B practices a skill already introduced in Phase A, use active power-building language ("sharpen," "strengthen," "level up") not exploration language ("discover," "find out"). The child already knows WHAT to do — Phase B is WHERE they get stronger at it.

### Rule: Instruction Budget > Atmosphere Budget
For mental-skill modules, spend word budget on clarity of instruction (what to do, in what order) rather than mood-setting imagery. Compress atmosphere to one sentence maximum. The Preview section replaces atmosphere as the primary word investment.

## Clinical Notes

### Technique Delivery Check
The script delivers cognitive defusion (Hayes/ACT) via:
- Observer activation: "I wonder what my next thought will be?" (Tolle) ✅
- Thought-as-object recognition: "ah-ha! I found a thought!" (ACT defusion marker) ✅
- Non-engagement: "let it go right away, don't pay it any more attention" ✅
- Return to observer stance: "put your attention back on the job of watching" ✅

### Flag: "Stop Thinking About It"
The phrase "Then stop thinking about it" uses the word "stop" which the dossier flagged. However, in context this is "stop engaging with THIS thought and redirect" (regulation/redirection), not "stop all thoughts" (suppression). The immediately following line ("Put your attention back on the job of watching") makes the redirect explicit. **Clinical assessment: acceptable.** Alternative if preferred: "Then let it go."
