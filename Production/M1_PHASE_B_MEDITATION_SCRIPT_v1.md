## The Script

### WELCOME
Ahh... you've come to learn the magic of Calm. Good.

### CONNECTION
You saw the breath travel all the way down to the belly — that's where the magic lives. Now it's your turn.

### SETUP
Put your hand right on your belly. You can feel yourself breathing in and out. Here's what I want you to do.

### INSTRUCTION
Breathe in slowly through your nose... and feel your belly push your hand up.

Now let it out... nice and slow... and feel your belly come back down.

That's it. Let's do it again. Breathe in... your belly rises...

...and breathe out... your belly falls... nice and easy.

### DEEPENING
Keep going just like that. In... and out...

If your brain starts thinking about other things, that's OK. Just feel your belly again.

In... and out...

One more. Nice and slow. In... and out...

### LANDING
That feeling right there... that calm... that's your magic.

### EXIT
Stay right there. Just keep breathing.

## Voice Direction Notes

**Welcome:** The "Ahh..." is not a word — it's a warm, pleased exhale. The narrator has been waiting for the child. "Good." is its own beat, delivered with quiet satisfaction. Not "good job" — more like "good, you're here."

**Setup:** "Put your hand right on your belly" is a direct, gentle instruction — not a suggestion. The child needs to do this now. "Here's what I want you to do" shifts energy from context to action — slight forward lean in the voice.

**Instruction:** Breathing instructions sync with real breath timing. The narrator inhales with "Breathe in slowly through your nose..." and the pause after the ellipsis is a real inhale (~3 seconds). "Now let it out..." is spoken on the exhale. The extended exhale gets more verbal space (Porges vagal mechanism). "Nice and slow" and "nice and easy" are delivered at actual exhale pace.

**Deepening:** "In... and out..." — the narrator says less, the pauses grow slightly. The child is doing it themselves now. "If your brain starts thinking about other things, that's OK" — delivered matter-of-fact, not overly gentle. This is normal, not a problem.

**Landing:** "That feeling right there..." — soft. Almost a whisper. "That calm..." — name it. "That's your magic." — quiet certainty. This is the moment where the child feels what they just did.

**Exit:** "Stay right there. Just keep breathing." — trails off. Does not conclude. The Rescue sustain picks up seamlessly.

## Clinical Grounding

### Technique
Diaphragmatic breathing activating parasympathetic response via vagal tone (Porges, Polyvagal Theory). Slow breath drawn into the lower lungs causes the diaphragm to descend, stimulating the vagus nerve. Extended exhale shifts autonomic balance from sympathetic (fight/flight) to parasympathetic (rest/social engagement).

### Source Traceability

| Script line | Clinical source |
|---|---|
| "Breathe in slowly through your nose" | Kabat-Zinn, Thich Nhat Hanh, Generation Mindful consensus |

| "Let it out... nice and slow" | Porges (extended exhale = vagal activation), Kabat-Zinn ("on each out-breath, letting go") |
| "That's it. Let's do it again." | Kaiser Greenland (playful encouragement), Kabat-Zinn (repetition without pressure) |

| "If your brain starts thinking... that's OK" | Kabat-Zinn (non-striving, mind-wandering normalization) |
| "Just feel your belly again" | Kabat-Zinn (gentle return without judgment) |
| "That feeling right there... that's your magic" | Hanson (savoring the good), Saltzman ("Do you feel it? Good.") |

### Design Decisions

1. **No counting** — reserved for Module 2 (4-7-8); Kabat-Zinn supports no counting for beginners
2. **No breath-holding** — Generation Mindful trauma consensus: avoid with children
3. **Extended exhale emphasis via pacing** — more verbal space on out-breath (Porges vagal mechanism)
4. **Hand on belly as primary anchor** — proprioceptive feedback loop (Porges, Kaiser Greenland)
5. **One mind-wandering beat** — Kabat-Zinn (gentle return), Kaiser Greenland (normalize)
6. **"Brain" not "mind"** — age-appropriate (Siegel & Bryson), concrete body-part word
7. **"That's your magic" landing** — bridges Phase A vocabulary to Rescue; Hanson savoring principle
