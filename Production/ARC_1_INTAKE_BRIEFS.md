## MODULE: M1
**ARC:** 1  
**CREATURE:** Tessa  
**SPELL:** Magic Hands Spell  
**DOMAIN:** bodysensing  
**TECHNIQUE:** Palm Interoception / Energy Hands  
**TYPE:** New Spell  
**EVENT ORDER:** Event 1 (Play Order)

### NARRATIVE CONTEXT
The child first arrives in Everdale through the Guide Bird. The MindfulNest is dark and silent — all six runestones are dormant from the Great Storm. Tessa, a gentle turtle who has been guarding the Heartwood, trembles when the child appears. The child's first act of magic: touching Tessa's cold, frightened hands and warming them back to life. As the child generates tingling sensations in their own palms through the Magic Hands technique, Tessa feels the warmth and slowly comes back to stillness. This is the child's first runestone activation: Body Stone, orange.

### CLINICAL BASIS
Palm interoception — noticing tingling, warmth, and subtle sensations in the hands — builds the child's capacity to detect bodily sensations. This Tier 1 technique (impossible to miss sensation) creates immediate, tangible proof that "my mind controls my body." Front-loaded in Arc 1 for high-impact opening. Based on somatic experiencing (van der Kolk) and polyvagal theory (Porges). Sources: Fogel (2009), Mehling et al. (2012), Craig (2015), Kaiser Greenland (2010).

### PROPOSED PHASE B APPROACH
This is a foundational body-sensing technique where the child feels real tingling sensations in their hands. Phase B script should emphasize: (1) prep frame instructing the child to notice their hands and the sensations they already contain, (2) guided hand rubbing or tapping to generate tingling, (3) transition to Tessa responding to the warmth/tingling the child created. Potential visualization: helping Tessa's hands feel better by sharing warmth. The key is to name what's already happening ("Can you feel that tingling?") rather than asking the child to imagine something new.

### FLAGS
None. Foundational module. Clear narrative role and high-sensation technique. Inscription "Feel what's real" is literal — the child feels real sensations, and that reality IS the magic.

## MODULE: M2
**ARC:** 1  
**CREATURE:** Luna  
**SPELL:** Breath-Squeezers Spell  
**DOMAIN:** watching  
**TECHNIQUE:** Squeeze-and-Release / Progressive Tension Release  
**TYPE:** New Spell  
**EVENT ORDER:** Event 2 (Play Order)

### NARRATIVE CONTEXT
Luna the Owl has been searching for the MindfulNest — an academic obsession that defined her research. She discovers it with archaeological equipment in hand, and her excitement is physical — gripping, hunching, shaking with intensity. When she reads the inscription "Stay loose and light," she realizes her own body is the opposite of what the riddle describes. She's wound up so tight she can't hold her magnifying glass steady or think clearly. The Breath-Squeezers Spell teaches her to squeeze ALL the way tight (lean into the tension) and then release — and in that release, she understands the inscription and wakes the Watching Stone.

### CLINICAL BASIS
Squeeze-and-Release / Progressive Tension Release operates on two levels: (1) Physiological — the post-contraction release triggers a rebound parasympathetic response, dropping tension below baseline. (2) Attentional — the watching domain frames this as observation of the contrast between tight and loose. This is interoceptive contrast training. Tier 1 sensation — impossible to miss. Sources: Jacobson (1938), Öst (1987), Bernstein & Borkovec (1973), Kabat-Zinn (1990).

### PROPOSED PHASE B APPROACH
This is a physical tension-release technique with a strong contrasting mechanism. Phase B script should emphasize: (1) the child deliberately squeezing specific muscle groups (hands, arms, shoulders, whole body) as tight as possible, (2) holding the squeeze for a beat, (3) releasing all at once and noticing the immediate difference. Potential challenge: children may not hold the squeeze long enough to get the rebound effect — Guide Bird should coach: "Squeeze it ALL the way." The sensation language is explicit: "Can you feel the difference between tight and loose?" This is noticing, not feeling calm.

### FLAGS
None. Clear integration type (Access Support) — Luna already knows she needs to relax, the technique removes the physical block. The inscription discovery follows naturally from the release.

## MODULE: M4
**ARC:** 1  
**CREATURE:** Ember  
**SPELL:** Heart-Sending Spell  
**DOMAIN:** kindness  
**TECHNIQUE:** Loving-Kindness / Compassion  
**TYPE:** New Spell  
**EVENT ORDER:** Event 3 (Play Order)

### NARRATIVE CONTEXT
Ember arrives from Foxhollow carrying a Sweetrose sprout — a living thing that needs warmth to grow. The inscription reads "Let the flowers bloom." Luna's academic intelligence can't solve this riddle; Ember's instinctive warmth and connection to living things is what's needed. But the Sweetrose won't take root just anywhere — it needs a magically warm spot. The child's Heart-Sending Spell creates the warmth. The Sweetrose wreath grows around the MindfulNest, making it visually more beautiful, and wakes the Heart Stone.

### CLINICAL BASIS
Loving-Kindness / Compassion is metta-based warmth-sending. The child directs warm, kind feelings outward toward a specific location (the MindfulNest). The mechanism is attentional: activation of the insula and temporal-parietal junction, increased vagal tone, broadened prosocial attention. For a child, the metaphor is concrete: your warm feelings can make a place safe enough for a flower to grow. Sources: Salzberg (1995), Neff (2011), Fredrickson et al. (2008), Germer (2009).

### PROPOSED PHASE B APPROACH
This is an externally-directed kindness technique focused on creating warmth for growth. Phase B script should emphasize: (1) the child generating warm, kind feelings, (2) "sending" those feelings to a specific location (the magical spot where the Sweetrose will grow), (3) the physical sensation of warmth radiating from the child's heart or hands. Potential visualization: imagining warm light flowing from the child to the MindfulNest, creating a safe nest for the Sweetrose. This is fundamentally about care-as-action, not self-comfort.

### FLAGS
None. Clear Task Facilitation integration — the technique enables an external goal (creating conditions for planting). The visual payoff (wreath blooming around the MindfulNest) is the module's emotional anchor.

## MODULE: M6
**ARC:** 1  
**CREATURE:** Bramble  
**SPELL:** Humming Spell  
**DOMAIN:** breathing  
**TECHNIQUE:** Humming Breath / Vocal Vibration (Bhramari-derived)  
**TYPE:** New Spell  
**EVENT ORDER:** Event 4 (Play Order)

### NARRATIVE CONTEXT
Bramble approaches the MindfulNest like a practical problem — knocking, tapping, pushing to understand its state. He discovers that the Heartwood is "out of tune" and needs vibration to come alive. His solo humming produces only a faint response. The inscription says "Everything is made of energy" — literally, energy IS vibration. The child's Humming Spell fills the Heartwood's chamber with sustained vibration, resonating through the ancient wood like a tuning fork. This awakens the Calm Stone and produces an A4 consequence: the vibration dislodges old bark, revealing a hole in the Heartwood.

### CLINICAL BASIS
Humming Breath produces three simultaneous effects: (1) Prolonged exhalation activates the parasympathetic nervous system through vagal tone, (2) Vibration is felt throughout the chest, throat, sinuses, and skull — Tier 1 sensation, impossible to miss, (3) Sound creates an auditory anchor holding attention in the present moment. The child doesn't need to count breaths, follow a rhythm, or visualize. Just hum. Sources: Porges (2011), Kalyani et al. (2011), Brown & Gerbarg (2012), Goldsby et al. (2017).

### PROPOSED PHASE B APPROACH
This is a rich, multi-sensory technique driven by sustained exhalation and vibration. Phase B script should emphasize: (1) the child taking a breath in and humming on the exhale (sustained "Hmmmm"), (2) directing the hum toward the Heartwood, (3) feeling the vibration in their own chest and throat as they hum. Potential challenge: some children may feel self-conscious humming aloud — frame it as "ancient magic" that requires the child's whole body to participate. The sensation is vibration and sound, not calm (even though the technique is calming physiologically). The narrative frame is "tuning" not "relaxing."

### FLAGS
None. Clear Task Facilitation integration — the technique enables an external goal (awakening the Heartwood). The A4 consequence (hole discovery) is a strong narrative payoff that sets up Event 5.

## MODULE: M3
**ARC:** 1  
**CREATURE:** Benson  
**SPELL:** Brave Sniffing Spell  
**DOMAIN:** courage  
**TECHNIQUE:** Physiological Sigh / Double-Inhale Extended Exhale  
**TYPE:** New Spell  
**EVENT ORDER:** Event 5 (Play Order)

### NARRATIVE CONTEXT
Benson is lost, separated from his family, and terrified. He hides in a burrow but wants to help bring magic into the world. The inscription says "There is nothing to fear when you go inside" — and Oliver asks Benson to hop inside the Heartwood's hole to find what's there. Benson is paralyzed by fear, but not by trauma or threat — just by the overwhelm of the unfamiliar. The Brave Sniffing Spell (two quick sniffs followed by a long exhale) gives him a physical tool. He doesn't stop being scared; he becomes able to move WHILE scared. He enters the Heartwood and discovers a secret inscription inside.

### CLINICAL BASIS
Physiological Sigh is the fastest known non-pharmacological method for reducing acute physiological stress. The double inhale reinflates the alveoli, maximizing gas exchange surface area. The extended exhale activates the parasympathetic nervous system through vagal stimulation, slowing the heart rate and signaling safety to the brainstem. The net effect is immediately felt — racing heart slows. For Benson (a bunny nose-breather), the quick sniffing motion is instinctive and natural. "Brave Sniffing" is literal, not metaphorical. Sources: Huberman Lab (2021), Ramirez et al. (2023), Vlemincx et al. (2013), Porges (2011).

### PROPOSED PHASE B APPROACH
This is a rapid-reset technique with immediate physiological payoff. Phase B script should emphasize: (1) the child taking two quick, sharp sniffs through the nose (sniff-sniff), (2) followed by one long, slow exhale through the mouth, (3) noticing the shift in the body's response (slowing heart rate, settling). Potential challenge: the quick sniffs may feel unnatural compared to other breathing techniques — frame them as "brave sniffs" and anchor them to bunny physiology. The sensation language is somatic: "Feel how your heartbeat slows down." The emotional frame is "you can do brave things even when you're scared."

### FLAGS
None. Clear Personal Healing integration — the technique directly resolves Benson's fear response so he can take action. The secret inscription "The Snow Cats Guard the Source of Them All" is a Planted Question with payoff in Arc 2+.

## MODULE: M5
**ARC:** 1  
**CREATURE:** Bork  
**SPELL:** Letting Go Spell  
**DOMAIN:** selfgrounding  
**TECHNIQUE:** Acceptance / Stop-Fighting  
**TYPE:** New Spell  
**EVENT ORDER:** Event 6 (Play Order)

### NARRATIVE CONTEXT
Bork is an Enlightened Fireholder sent by the Luminara Swarm to establish a connection between the swarm and the runestones. He knows exactly what to do — connect his light to the final (sixth) runestone, the Grounding Stone. But his pride, duty, and effort to succeed make him push harder and harder, and every push makes his light flicker out. The inscription says "Connect with the Light" — and Bork learns this doesn't mean force your light into the stone, it means let your light be what it already is. When he stops trying, his bioluminescence blooms naturally, merges with the Grounding Stone, and ignites all six runestones in a cascade of color, restoring Everdale to full magical life.

### CLINICAL BASIS
Acceptance / Stop-Fighting operates on a paradoxical mechanism: the harder you try to relax, the more tense you become; the harder you push for a result, the more it eludes you. Acceptance interrupts the efforting cycle by teaching release of the demand that things be different. Physiologically, the release of muscular and cognitive efforting allows the parasympathetic system to do what it already knows how to do. For Bork, bioluminescence is natural, innate, and automatic — forcing it makes it sputter; letting go lets it shine. Sources: Hayes et al. (2006), Kabat-Zinn (1990), Linehan (1993), Forsyth & Eifert (2016).

### PROPOSED PHASE B APPROACH
This is the inverse of every other technique in Arc 1: instead of DOING, this is NOT doing. Phase B script should emphasize: (1) the child releasing the demand to "do it right," (2) letting their magic be what it already is without forcing, (3) noticing what happens when they stop trying. Potential challenge: this is subtle compared to body-sensing, tension release, and breathing techniques. Bork's visual payoff (his glow blooming bigger and bigger) carries the narrative — the child feels the shift internally as a loosening of effort, not as a tangible sensation. The emotional frame is "sometimes letting go is the strongest move."

### FLAGS
None. Clear Access Support integration — Bork already knows what to do and has tried, the technique removes the block of effortful control. This is the final module, so the visual payoff is the largest: all six runestones ignite simultaneously, Everdale fully restored. Strong thematic closure.
