# Phase B Clarity Checklist

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** April 1, 2026
**Purpose:** The pre-draft clarity gate for Phase B meditation scripts. Run this checklist AFTER the Research Dossier (Step 0) and Language Audit (Step 2), BEFORE drafting the script (Step 3). If you can't answer every question cleanly, you're not ready to write.
**Integrates with:** PHASE_B_PRODUCTION_PROCESS_v1_2.md (slots between Steps 2 and 3)

---

## The Core Principle

Phase A is "watch." Phase B is "try."

The child just watched a creature learn a technique. Now they try it themselves. The entire Phase B script exists to make ONE thing crystal clear: **what the child's body or mind is supposed to DO.**

Everything else — section structure, pacing, word count, silence vs. guidance — follows from how clear you are about that one thing.

---

## The Four Questions

Before writing a single word of script, answer these. If any answer is muddy, the script will be muddy.

### Q1: What did the child just WATCH?

Pull the exact Phase A vocabulary. What metaphor did they see? What words did Guide Bird use? What physical action did the creature demonstrate?

This is Step 2 (Language Audit) from the Production Process. You should already have a vocabulary card. The point here is sharper: **in one sentence, what did the child see happen?**

> **M1 example:** The child watched Tessa's belly rise and fall with each breath — the breath travels down to the belly, and the belly pushes up.
>
> **M2 example:** The child watched the breathing circle count out a 4-7-8 rhythm — 4 in, 7 hold, 8 out, with the long breath out as the magic part.
>
> **M3 example:** The child watched thoughts float by like clouds — and saw that you can choose which ones get your attention.

### Q2: What is the child now going to TRY?

One concrete action. Physical if possible. If it's a mental action, it must be concrete enough that a 7-year-old could explain it to a friend.

**Test:** Say it out loud. If you need more than one sentence, you're not clear enough yet.

> **M1:** Breathe into your belly so your hand rises, then let it out so your hand falls.
>
> **M2:** Breathe along while I count — 4 in, 7 hold, 8 out.
>
> **M3:** Watch for a thought to appear, say "ah-ha!" when you spot one, notice what it's about, then let it go and watch for the next one.

Note how M3's answer is longer — because the action is invisible. The child can't feel "watching thoughts" the way they can feel their belly move. That length tells you the script needs more setup (see Q2b below).

### Q2b: Can the child FEEL it happening?

This is the structural fork. It determines how much setup your script needs.

**If YES (body-based technique):** The child gets physical feedback — belly moves, muscles squeeze and release, vibration in chest from humming. The script can be simple: do the thing, notice the thing, keep doing the thing. Standard 7-section structure works.

> M1: YES — hand on belly rises and falls. Script is 155 words, standard structure.
> M2: YES — they feel their lungs fill, hold, release. Script provides the counting structure and the body does the rest.

**If NO (mental/cognitive technique):** The child can't see or feel the technique. The script needs to invest word budget in making the invisible visible:

- **Add a PREVIEW section** — walk through every step of the exercise before "Let's begin" so the child has a complete mental map
- **Add an ACTION MARKER** — give the child a specific playful thing to do at the key therapeutic moment ("ah-ha!" for defusion, "found it!" for sense anchor)
- **Frame it as a JOB** — "the job of watching thoughts" gives the child a concrete role instead of abstract observation

> M3: NO — watching thoughts is invisible. Script adds PREVIEW (full step-by-step walkthrough), "ah-ha!" action marker, "job of watching" framing. 170 words — the extra budget goes entirely to clarity of instruction, not atmosphere.

**If PARTIALLY (technique starts physical, goes internal):** Start with the physical anchor, then bridge to the internal practice. The physical part orients the child before the subtler work begins.

### Q3: How will the child KNOW it's working?

What does the child notice that tells them the technique is active? This becomes the LANDING beat — the moment where you name what they just experienced.

**Rules:**
- Must be something the child can actually notice (not something only a clinician would observe)
- Use conditional language — "that feeling right there" not "you now feel calm"
- Name it in Everdale terms — "that's your magic"

> **M1:** Their belly is moving with their breath, and they feel calm. → "That feeling right there... that calm... that's your magic."
>
> **M2:** Their body feels different — deeper, tingly, relaxed — after the counted breathing. → "That deep... tingly feeling of relaxation... that's your magic coming through."
>
> **M3:** They caught a thought, named it, and let it go — they're getting stronger at choosing where attention goes. → "Watching your thoughts makes you stronger and stronger."

### Q4: What will a 7-year-old get WRONG?

The most common failure mode for this age group. The script must preempt it without making the child feel like they're failing.

**Rules:**
- Address it matter-of-factly, not sympathetically
- Normalize it — "that's OK" or "that happens"
- Redirect to the core action immediately after normalizing

> **M1:** Chest breathing instead of belly breathing. → Script uses hand-on-belly as the physical anchor so the child can self-correct. No need to say "you might be breathing wrong."
>
> **M2:** Rushing the count, straining on the hold, running out of air on the exhale. → Script has the narrator count aloud (child doesn't have to count silently), and uses "Hold it for" (ease) not "Hold your breath" (strain).
>
> **M3:** Trying to STOP thoughts instead of watching them. → Script says "Stop thinking about it" (redirect) immediately followed by "Put your attention back on the job of watching" (return to observer stance). The action marker ("ah-ha!") reframes thought-catching as a game, not a battle.

## From Answers to Structure

Once you've answered Q1-Q4, the script structure reveals itself:

### If Q2b = YES (child can feel it):

Use the **standard 7-section template:**

```
WELCOME     →  "Ahh... [recognition]. Good."
CONNECTION  →  "You saw [Q1 answer]. Now it's your turn."
SETUP       →  Physical positioning instruction
INSTRUCTION →  Guide the action from Q2. Repeat 2-3x with variation.
DEEPENING   →  Less guidance, more child independence. Address Q4 if needed.
LANDING     →  Name Q3: "That feeling... that's your magic."
EXIT        →  "Stay right there. Just keep [core action]."
```

Word budget: ~120-160 words. Spend most on INSTRUCTION and DEEPENING.

### If Q2b = NO (child can't feel it):

Use the **preview-enhanced template:**

```
WELCOME        →  "Ahh... [recognition]. Good."
CONNECTION     →  "You saw [Q1 answer]. Let's [sharpen/strengthen] that power."
PREVIEW        →  Walk through every step: "I will ask you to... then you will...
                   then you will..." (the child gets a complete map before starting)
SETUP          →  Physical positioning + "Let's begin."
INSTRUCTION    →  Guide the first attempt. Use ACTION MARKER at key moment.
CHECK-IN       →  Brief pause + "Have you noticed/felt/found one?"
GUIDED PRACTICE → Repeat with the child doing more independently. Frame as JOB.
LANDING        →  Name Q3 using strength/power language.
EXIT           →  "Stay right there. Just keep [core action]..."
```

Word budget: ~150-180 words. Spend most on PREVIEW and INSTRUCTION. Compress atmosphere to one sentence max — word budget goes to clarity, not mood.

### If the technique has a COUNTED RHYTHM:

Use the **cycle-based template:**

```
WELCOME     →  "Ahh... [recognition]. Good."
CONNECTION  →  "You saw [Q1 answer]. Now it's your turn."
SETUP       →  "I'm going to [count/guide]. All you have to do is [breathe/follow] along."
CYCLE 1     →  Full guided cycle with narrator counting/pacing aloud.
TRANSITION  →  Brief acknowledgment: "Good... [somatic observation]... One more."
CYCLE 2     →  Identical structure to Cycle 1 (consistency reduces cognitive load).
LANDING     →  Name Q3: somatic noticing + "that's your magic."
EXIT        →  "Stay right there."
```

Word budget: ~120-140 words. The counting IS the instruction — no unstructured silence needed. No DEEPENING section (counting continuously occupies attention). Keep both cycles structurally identical.

## The Constants (Every Script, Every Archetype)

These are non-negotiable across all Phase B scripts:

**WELCOME** always:
- Opens with "Ahh..." (warm exhale, not a word)
- Ends with "Good." (its own beat — quiet satisfaction, not praise)
- Has character — the narrator is a person, not a voice

**CONNECTION** always:
- References Phase A using exact Phase A vocabulary
- Frames the shift from watching to doing
- Never re-teaches what Phase A already showed

**LANDING** always:
- Names the experience in Everdale terms
- Uses "that's your magic" or equivalent ownership language
- Never evaluates performance (no "good job" — that's for Rescue/Win)

**EXIT** always:
- Says "Stay right there"
- Trails off — does NOT conclude
- The Rescue sustain picks up seamlessly

**Voice direction** always:
- Narrator breathes WITH the child, not AT them
- Warm, wise, unhurried — grandparent energy
- No therapy-speak ("regulate," "coping," "therapeutic")
- Conditional language for feelings ("notice if" not "you will feel")
- "You" never "we" — narrator guides, doesn't participate

## Anti-Patterns (Quick Reference)

These kill Phase B scripts. Check for them after drafting:

| Don't | Do Instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Spend words on atmosphere/imagery | Spend words on instruction clarity | Kids need to know what to DO, not feel a vibe |
| Use exploration language ("discover," "find out") for returning skills | Use power language ("sharpen," "strengthen") | Phase B isn't discovery — the child already knows WHAT from Phase A |
| Leave silence >8 seconds without guidance | Guide verbally every 5-8 seconds | 7-year-olds interpret silence as "am I doing this wrong?" |
| Promise feelings ("you'll feel calm") | Focus on process ("just keep breathing") | Sets up failure if they don't feel it |
| Add new vocabulary not in Phase A | Use only the vocabulary card words | Language consistency = trust |
| Write compound instructions | One instruction per sentence | Cognitive load — one thing at a time |
| Use "we" | Use "you" | Narrator guides; child practices |
