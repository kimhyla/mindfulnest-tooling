# Phase B Research Dossier Process
## For Modules 1-12 (Existing Designs)

### Purpose

Before writing any Phase B meditation script, build a research
dossier for that module by systematically searching our 26 clinical
sources for guidance on the specific technique that module teaches.

Phase A is already locked. The metaphor is chosen. The vocabulary
is set. This dossier does NOT question Phase A's design. It asks:
"Given what Phase A established, what do our clinical sources tell
us about the best way to guide a child through this actual practice
with their eyes closed?"

The dossier is the raw material. The Phase B Production Process
(separate document) shapes it into a script.

---

### Step 0a — Identify Relevant Sources

From the 26 clinical traditions in §11 of the Authoring Guide,
identify which sources are relevant to THIS module's specific
technique. Typically 5-10 sources per module.

**Selection criteria:**
- Primary source for the technique (e.g., Porges for belly breathing)
- Sources that have published guided practice scripts for children
  using this technique (e.g., Kaiser Greenland, Saltzman)
- Sources that describe the technique's mechanism in enough detail
  to inform guided instruction (e.g., van der Kolk for body awareness)
- Child-specific sources that address age-appropriate adaptation
  (e.g., Generation Mindful, Siegel & Bryson)
- Sources whose general principles apply (e.g., Ross Greene's
  non-coercive philosophy, Hanson's "taking in the good")

**Output:** A numbered list of relevant sources with a one-line note
on WHY each is relevant to this module.

---

### Step 0b — Systematic Source Research

For each relevant source identified in 0a, search comprehensively
for the following categories of information. Not every source will
have content in every category — that's expected.

#### Category 1: Guided Practice Sequences
How does this source guide the actual technique? What steps do they
follow? What order? What comes first, second, third? Look for
published scripts, audio transcriptions, curriculum guides, workshop
descriptions.

**What we're looking for:**
- Step-by-step guided practice instructions
- Recommended sequence/order of instructions
- Timing recommendations (how long per step, total duration)
- Whether they use counting, rhythm, silence, imagery

#### Category 2: Child-Specific Language
What words and phrases does this source use when teaching this
technique to children? What vocabulary do they recommend? What
vocabulary do they warn against?

**What we're looking for:**
- Specific phrases used with children ages 5-10
- Vocabulary recommendations and warnings
- How they simplify adult concepts for children
- Metaphors they use (and whether they match or conflict
  with our Phase A metaphor)

#### Category 3: Physical Cues and Body Positioning
What does this source say about the child's body during practice?
Hands where? Eyes open or closed? Sitting or lying? What physical
anchors do they use?

**What we're looking for:**
- Recommended body position for this technique
- Physical anchors (hand on belly, hand on heart, etc.)
- Whether eyes should be open or closed (and for how long)
- Movement vs. stillness during practice

#### Category 4: Common Failure Modes
What does this source say about how children (or beginners) get
this technique wrong? What mistakes are common? What
misconceptions arise?

**What we're looking for:**
- Most common errors for this age group
- Misconceptions about what the technique does
- Signs that the child is doing it wrong
- How to correct without creating performance pressure

#### Category 5: Therapeutic Mechanism
What does this source say about WHY this technique works? What is
the underlying mechanism? What physiological or psychological
process is being activated?

**What we're looking for:**
- Mechanism of action (physiological, cognitive, emotional)
- What the child's nervous system/brain is doing during practice
- Why the specific instructions matter (not just what, but why)
- What would be lost if we simplified too much

#### Category 6: Pacing and Silence
What does this source say about timing, pauses, silence during
guided practice with children? How much verbal guidance vs. quiet?

**What we're looking for:**
- Recommended frequency of verbal guidance for children
- How they handle silence (do they fill it? leave it?)
- Pacing recommendations (slower? faster? match the child?)
- Whether they use counting or timing cues

#### Category 7: Cautions and Contraindications
What does this source warn against? What should the guide NOT do?
What could go wrong?

**What we're looking for:**
- What NOT to say during this technique
- Populations or situations where this technique needs modification
- Emotional reactions that might arise
- How to handle a child who can't do it or gets upset

---

### Step 0c — Compile the Dossier

Organize all findings into a single document per module:

```
# Phase B Research Dossier: Module [N] — [Name]
## Technique: [specific technique name]
## Phase A Metaphor: [what Phase A established]
## Phase A Vocabulary: [key words from language audit]

### Relevant Sources (from §11)
1. [Source] — [why relevant]
2. ...

### Findings by Source

#### [Source 1 Name]
- Guided practice sequences: ...
- Child-specific language: ...
- Physical cues: ...
- Common failure modes: ...
- Therapeutic mechanism: ...
- Pacing notes: ...
- Cautions: ...

#### [Source 2 Name]
...

### Cross-Source Synthesis

#### Points of Agreement
(Where do multiple sources agree on approach?)

#### Points of Tension
(Where do sources disagree? Which do we follow and why?)

#### Phase A Alignment Check
(Do any source recommendations conflict with what Phase A
already established? If so, flag as potential mismatch.)

#### Recommended Approach
(Based on all sources, what approach should the Phase B
script take? Cite which sources support each decision.)

#### Elements to Incorporate
(Specific language, sequences, metaphors, physical cues
that should appear in the script, with source attribution.)

#### Elements to Avoid
(Specific patterns warned against by sources, with
source attribution.)
```

---

### Step 0d — Review and Validate

Before proceeding to Step 1 of the Phase B Production Process:

- [ ] At least 3 sources consulted per module
- [ ] Primary clinical source for the technique included
- [ ] At least 1 child-specific source included
- [ ] Cross-source synthesis identifies agreements and tensions
- [ ] Phase A alignment check completed — no unresolved conflicts
- [ ] Recommended approach is traceable to cited sources
- [ ] No recommendations based solely on general knowledge
      (everything attributed)
